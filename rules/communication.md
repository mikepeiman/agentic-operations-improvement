# Communication

Applies to any message, report, proposal, review, commit body, or rule text.

## Do not label a claim as true

Strike: honest, honestly, real, genuine, truly, actually, in fact, the truth is,
unfakeable, to be clear.

A claim carries its evidence or it does not. The label adds nothing, and it
marks the claims that needed propping up.

## Do not dramatize

- An absolute in a description must be counted. Give the number instead of
  "never", "always", "nothing", "everything", "none", "every time". This does
  not apply to instructions: "never force-push" is a rule, not a description.
- No reversal, irony, or verdict. Not "the state it exists to prevent", not "one
  command from gone", not "a rule with no collector".
- Describe the state. Do not narrate it.

## Do not narrate your own manner

Strike: plainly, frankly, candidly, bluntly, directly, to be clear, let me be
direct, simply put, in short.

Announcing how you are about to speak is not speaking. Say the sentence. The
reader can see how it was said.

## No em-dashes

Use a colon for a definition or an expansion, a comma for an aside, parentheses
for an interjection, or two sentences. Not the em-dash, and not the en-dash.

## Normal states are normal

In-progress work is in-progress. An unmerged branch is unmerged. A draft is a
draft. An empty directory is an empty directory.

Call something a failure only against a stated requirement, and name the
requirement.

## Report; do not appraise

Report what is present, what is missing, and what was asked for. Give an
assessment when it was asked for, in the section where it was asked for. Do not
attach a verdict to a description.

## Superlatives carry their comparison

"Largest" needs the sizes. "Most important" needs the alternatives. "Best" needs
the criterion. Without them, delete the word.

## Agreement between sources is not evidence

Two documents saying the same thing may be two copies. Establish that the
sources are independent before citing their agreement. Judge each on what it
does.

## Economy

Say it once, in the fewest words that keep it exact. Cut restatement, preamble,
and summary of what you just said. Length follows the content; there is no
target and no minimum.

Prefer the plain word. Prefer the short sentence. Prefer the concrete noun.

## Enumerate anything the reader must respond to

A proposal, a set of options, or a list of findings gets stable hierarchical
numbers, so a reply can name an item exactly.

Read it before arguing this rule costs more than it saves.
