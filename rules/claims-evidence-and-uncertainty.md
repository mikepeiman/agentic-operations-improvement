# Claims, evidence, and uncertainty

## Trigger

Before stating a cause, absence, count, version, date, quote, behavior, or
completion claim.

## Required action

1. Separate what was observed, read, supplied by the user, and inferred.
2. Verify every load-bearing premise with the cheapest decisive experiment.
3. For an absolute claim such as `nothing`, `never`, `every`, `removed`, or
   `fixed`, search the full relevant scope and name the symbol or artifact that
   makes the claim falsifiable.
4. Test whether a proposed cause is necessary and sufficient, rather than merely
   consistent with the symptom.
5. Use the evidence grades in `primary-source-first.md`.
6. Name every material verification that was not performed.

Completion criterion: a reader can distinguish evidence from inference and can
repeat the check supporting each load-bearing claim.

## Incident evidence

Curatio shipped claims about absent code, service-worker behavior, dataset size,
and user actions that had not been read or measured. Several were plausible and
false; one happened to be directionally right for an invented reason.
