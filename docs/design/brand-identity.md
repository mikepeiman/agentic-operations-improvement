Use for logos, app marks, favicons, wordmarks, visual identities, icon systems, brand refreshes, and identity exploration.

## Guidance

Do not design a logo in isolation. Consider the identity system: icon, wordmark, typography, color, small-size use, monochrome use, app/browser icon, website, social avatar, and surrounding visual language.

Research both the project's category and adjacent categories so the result is recognizable without becoming generic.

Inspect multiple strong identities before generating directions. Identify useful principles—geometry, rhythm, negative space, typographic personality, symbol construction—not individual marks to imitate.

Prioritize:
- distinct silhouette,
- recognizability,
- reproduction at very small sizes,
- simplicity of form,
- meaningful relationship to the product,
- flexibility across light/dark and physical/digital contexts.

Avoid unnecessary gradients, tiny internal detail, meaningless complexity, and literal visual clichés unless they are transformed into something distinctive.

Generate genuinely different concepts, not the same mark with minor styling variations.

**Working principle:** create a simple, ownable core mark that can support a richer identity system around it.
